# NASA RASSOR Digital Twin Rover

> **UCF Senior Design | Florida Space Institute-supported lunar rover project**

This repository documents my work on a multidisciplinary senior design project at the University of Central Florida focused on building a **RASSOR-inspired lunar rover** and supporting a remote-operation/digital-twin system for lunar exploration.

The project combined a physical rover, remote operation, sensing, and a digital representation of the rover and its environment. My primary responsibility was **Assembly and Development**, with additional contributions to technical research and documentation.

## Project Goal

Operating a rover on the lunar surface presents a major challenge because commands and feedback cannot always be exchanged instantaneously between Earth and the rover. Our senior design project explored a system in which a physical rover could be remotely operated while a digital twin provided a virtual representation of the rover and its environment.

The project progressed from defining the communication and teleoperation problem, through physical rover construction and testing, to integration with the team's digital-twin and sensing work.

## My Role — Assembly & Development

My main responsibility was getting the **physical rover assembled, integrated, diagnosed, and operational**.

### Physical Rover Assembly

I manually assembled the rover and its drivetrain components from the project's hardware and 3D-printed parts. This included working with the drive hubs, gearboxes, motors, structural components, and other mechanical parts required to turn the design into a functioning physical platform.

### Hardware Troubleshooting & Rebuild

During testing, the rover was unstable and was not moving as expected. I worked through the hardware to determine what was causing the problem rather than assuming it was purely a software issue.

My work included:

- Testing the physical rover and drivetrain to isolate hardware problems.
- Disassembling rover components to inspect the drive system.
- Identifying assembly issues affecting the drive hubs/gearing.
- Rebuilding components and correcting the mechanical fit of the drivetrain.
- Working with replacement/reprinted components when parts needed to be remade.
- Reassembling the rover and testing it again after the changes.

This became one of the most important parts of my experience on the project: taking a system that was not behaving correctly, diagnosing the physical cause, rebuilding it, and verifying whether the changes improved its operation.

### Electrical Setup & Integration

I also helped set up and integrate the rover's electrical components, including the motor/electronics connections required for the physical platform. This work supported subsequent rover movement, control, and testing by the team.

## Project Development

The senior-design work developed across three major reporting stages:

**1. Project Proposal** — defined the problem, proposed approach, requirements, constraints, standards, and initial plan.

**2. AS-IS** — documented the system as it existed during development, including the physical rover, electronics, communication approach, digital-twin work, risks, and broader impacts.

**3. TO-BE** — documented the completed/final system, testing, lessons learned, and the team's final implementation.

The final presentation brought those stages together and demonstrated the completed rover and digital-twin project.
